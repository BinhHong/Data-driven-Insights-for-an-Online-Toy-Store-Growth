# Insights and Business Recommendations


# Executive Summary

The business had a strong revenue growth over the analyzed period, driven primarily by increasing order volume. The drivers of order growth changed over time: early growth was supported by both rising traffic and improving conversion, much of 2013 was mainly acquisition-driven, and the second half of 2014 benefited from both higher traffic and stronger conversion.

Several findings stand out:

- Google is the dominant acquisition source and is responsible for most major traffic spikes, creating meaningful dependence on a single source.
- Desktop traffic noticeably outperforms mobile traffic in conversion and revenue per session. The gap persists within individual traffic sources and comparable landing pages, making device one of the strongest conversion-performance drivers identified.
- Repeat sessions are more valuable than new sessions, however business growth still relies primarily on acquisitions, because sessions per visitor are broadly stable.
- Bundle purchasing substantially increases transaction value. Bundles account for roughly one quarter of orders but more than one third of revenue, with an AOV approximately 76% higher than single item orders.
- The product portfolio became more diversified over time, but The Original Mr. Fuzzy remains the dominant revenue and profit contributor.
- Refund growth largely reflected sales growth, indicating that increasing refund amount were mostly scale driven rather than evidence of broad deterioration. However, a major September 2014 anomaly was concentrated in Mr. Fuzzy, while Birthday Sugar Panda exhibits the highest persistent refund rate.
- Raw landing page conversion rankings should not be interpreted as direct evidence of page effectiveness because page exposure differs by device mix and operating period.

The highest opportunities are therefore:

- Investigate and improve the mobile conversion journey.
- Protect growth from excessive dependence on Google by improving acquisition.
- Continue using bundle and cross-sell strategies to increase basket value.
- Investigate product-specific refund risk, especially Mr. Fuzzy anomalies and Sugar Panda's high refund rate.
- Grow higher-margin, lower-refund products where feasible.
- Use controlled testing before making landing-page optimization decisions.


# 1. Growth and Revenue Drivers

## Early revenue growth was driven primarily by order-volume expansion

Revenue and orders moved almost identically over time, while AOV remained relatively stable during long periods.

From March to November 2012:

- Sessions increased by approximately 646%.
- Conversion Rate improved by approximately 1.22 percentage points.
- Orders increased by approximately 930%.
- Revenue increased by approximately 930%.
- AOV remained approximately unchanged at $49.99.

This indicates that early growth was almost entirely volume-driven rather than driven by customers spending more per transaction.

## During 2014 conversion became a more important growth driver

From February to December 2014:

- Sessions increased by approximately 82.5%.
- Conversion improved by approximately 1.52 percentage points.
- Orders increased by approximately 126.6%.
- Revenue increased by approximately 119.4%.
- AOV declined slightly.

During July 2014 to February 2015, orders grew significantly faster than sessions while conversion continued to improve. After the December 2014 traffic peak, sessions fell by approximately 20% by February 2015, while orders and revenue declined by only around 11%. The stronger conversion rate partially offset the loss of traffic.

## Recommendation: Protect both acquisition volume and conversion efficiency

- Continue monitoring Sessions, Conversion Rate, Orders, and Revenue together rather than evaluating them independently.
- Separate acquisition-driven growth from conversion-driven growth in management reporting.
- Prioritize conversion optimization where the analysis identifies clear performance gaps, especially mobile.


# 2. Traffic Acquisition

## Google is the dominant acquisition source

Google accounts for approximately:

- 66.8% of all sessions.
- 66.0% of converted sessions.

Google also drove most of the major November traffic spikes:

- Approximately 72% of the Oct–Nov 2012 session increase.
- Approximately 84% of the Oct–Nov 2013 increase.
- Approximately 73% of the Oct–Nov 2014 increase.

By late 2014 the composition became somewhat more diverse, with unattributed traffic and Facebook accounting for a larger portion of sessions.

## Traffic volume and traffic quality are not the same

Google generates the most traffic, but Bing and unattributed traffic show slightly stronger conversion efficiency with 7.19% and 7.34% respectively compared to 6.75% from Google in Conversion Rate, $4.28 and 
$4.46 compared to $4.04 from Google in Revenue per Session.

## Recommendation: Reduce acquisition concentration while preserving Google scale

- Maintain Google as a core acquisition source because it remains the largest driver of traffic and conversions.
- Investigate whether higher-efficiency sources such as Bing can be scaled without losing conversion quality.
- Review Facebook traffic before allocating additional acquisition effort because its conversion and revenue-per-session performance are noticeably weaker.

# 3. Device Performance

## Mobile is the clearest conversion-efficiency gap

Desktop accounts for approximately 69% of sessions and 86% of converted sessions, Conversion Rate is approximately 8.50% and Revenue per Session is approximately $5.09, while for Mobile performance, Conversion Rate is approximately 3.09% and Revenue per Session is approximately $1.87.

Desktop therefore converts approximately 2.75 times better and generates approximately 2.7 times more revenue per session.

## The mobile gap persists after controlling for traffic source and landing page

Within every traffic source, desktop conversion exceeds mobile conversion by approximately 4–7 percentage points.

The same desktop advantage persists on landing pages that receive both desktop and mobile traffic.

## Recommendation: Prioritize a mobile conversion diagnostic
- Audit the mobile customer journey from landing page through product selection and checkout.
- Review mobile page speed, navigation, product-page usability, forms, cart behavior, and checkout disadvantages.

# 4. Visitor Behaviour

## Repeat sessions are smaller in volume but higher in value

Repeat sessions represent approximately 16.6% of sessions but about 19% of converted sessions. Compared with new sessions, repeat sessions convert approximately 1.2 percentage points better and generate roughly 19% more revenue per session.

## Overall traffic growth remained acquisition-led

Sessions per visitor remained broadly stable, generally around 1.03–1.06, while total sessions and unique visitors grew strongly. During 2014, sessions and unique visitors increased at almost the same rate.

## Conversion improvement was not mainly due to repeat-sessions

During the conversion improvementin late 2014, New-session conversion increased significantly, while Repeat-session conversion also increased and Repeat-session share increased only slightly.

The improvement in overall conversion was therefore driven primarily by higher conversion efficiency within both new and repeat sessions rather than by traffic shifting toward a higher-converting repeat-session mix.

## Recommendation: 

- Develop re-engagement strategies for returning visitors because repeat sessions show higher conversion and revenue efficiency.
- Continue investing in new visitor acquisition because overall traffic growth remains primarily acquisition-driven.

# 5. Product Portfolio

## The Original Mr. Fuzzy remains the dominant driver

The Original Mr. Fuzzy contributes approximately 60.5% of units sold, 62.5% of product revenue and 60.8% of gross profit. Its strength is driven primarily by scale rather than superior margin, which is approximately 61%, lower than several smaller products.

## Product diversification improved over time

At the beginning of the dataset, Mr. Fuzzy generated essentially all product revenue. After subsequent product launches, its monthly revenue share declined to roughly half by late 2014. This indicates that new products successfully broadened the revenue base.

## Smaller products have different margin and refund profiles

However, refund risk differs significantly:
- Birthday Sugar Panda has the highest product refund rate.
- Hudson River Mini Bear has the lowest refund rate while maintaining a high margin.

## Recommendation: Continue portfolio diversification using both margin and refund quality

- Reduce excessive dependence on Mr. Fuzzy by continuing to develop sales from other products.
- Evaluate growth opportunities for products combining strong margins with low refund exposure, particularly Mini Bear.
- Track revenue share, profit share, and refund rate together when evaluating product performance.

# 6. Basket and Bundle Behaviour

## Bundle purchases significantly increase transaction value

- Bundle purchases account for approximately 24% of orders and 36% of revenue.
- Bundle AOV is approximately 76% higher than Single-Item AOV

## The late-2013 and early-2014 AOV increase was basket-driven

Between November 2013 and February 2014:

- AOV increased from approximately $54.16 to $64.65.
- Bundle Order Share increased from approximately 4.3% to 32.0%.
- Units per Order increased from approximately 1.04 to 1.32.
- Average selling price per unit declined rather than increased.

The AOV increase was not caused primarily by customers switching toward more expensive individual products. It was driven by larger baskets and a sharp increase in bundle purchasing.

## Recommendation: Use bundle and cross-sell strategies to grow AOV

- Continue encouraging multi-item purchasing because bundle orders produce materially higher AOV.
- Evaluate which products are most effective as primary products and which function best as additional items.
- Monitor whether bundle growth improves revenue without creating a corresponding increase in refund exposure.

# 7. Refund Drivers

## Long-term refund growth was primarily scale-driven

From 2013 to 2014, Revenue increased by approximately 173.5% and Refund Amount increased by approximately 172.9%. The ratio of refund amount to revenue remained broadly stable at approximately 4.37% in both years.

## September 2014 was a refund anomaly concentraing on Mr. Fuzzy

- Approximately 90% of the month's refund amount were associated with The Original Mr. Fuzzy. Mr. Fuzzy units sold in August and September 2014 experienced eventual refund rates above 13%, compared with approximately 4% before and approximately 2.5% afterward.
- The spike appears temporary and product-specific rather than evidence of a wide deterioration.

## Mr. Fuzzy is the largest refund cost driver, while Sugar Panda presents the strongest refund rate. Mini Bear has the most favorable combined margin-refund profile.

## Recommendation: Investigate product-specific refund causes instead of treating refunds as a portfolio-wide problem

- Conduct a root-cause investigation into the August–September 2014 Mr. Fuzzy sales cohorts.
- Review quality-control, fulfillment, production-batch, packaging, and customer-service information if available.
- Investigate the high refund rate of Birthday Sugar Panda separately from the temporary Mr. Fuzzy anomaly.
- Monitor both Refund Amount and Product Refund Rate
- Preserve or expand low-refund, high-margin products where market demand supports growth.

# 8. Landing-Page Analysis

## Landing-page rankings are strongly affected by external factors

Performance shows large differences but:
- several pages were exposed to different device mixes;
- some pages were effectively device-specific;
- pages operated during different historical periods;
- overall conversion improved over time.

## Recommendation: Use controlled tests for landing-page decisions

- Compare landing pages only within overlapping time periods and comparable device populations.
- Avoid ranking page design solely on lifetime conversion averages.

# Final Business Conclusion

The business achieved substantial growth primarily through increasing traffic and order volume, with conversion efficiency becoming increasingly important over time.

The strongest opportunity identified is not simply to acquire more traffic. The evidence suggests that future growth quality can improve by:

- closing the persistent mobile conversion gap
- reducing acquisition dependence on a single dominant traffic source
- encouraging larger baskets through bundle and cross-sell behavior
- growing attractive products while considering refund exposure
- addressing product-specific refund anomalies and refund risk
- increasing repeat engagement without neglecting new visitor acquisition
- and using controlled testing before attributing performance differences to landing-page design.

The central strategy is therefore:
- Shift from growth driven mainly by traffic volume toward growth that combines acquisition scale with stronger conversion efficiency, higher basket value, healthier product economics, and controlled refund risk.
